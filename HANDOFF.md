## IDX LEDGER CROSS-CHECK (2026-08-11) — feasibility investigated, built

Answers the open feasibility question from the earlier BNBR/B-bucket
report: **yes, IDX exposes corporate-action-adjacent data as structured
JSON**, reachable through the same curl_cffi(`impersonate="chrome"`) +
Referer pattern already used for `GetCompanyProfiles`/`GetStockSummary`.
Endpoint: `https://www.idx.co.id/primary/ListingActivity/GetIssuedHistory`
— a share-count ledger (ticker, listing date, Indonesian action-type
code, shares added/outstanding-after), 1801 rows as of 2026-08-11,
spanning 1911-01-01 to today. Implemented in
`sources/idx_corporate_actions.py`, wired into
`jobs/generate_ca_review.py` as a cross-check (never applied to the DB,
same as everything else in that file).

**`TanggalPencatatan` (listing date) is NOT the ex-date** — verified by
matching 71 stockSplit/reverseStock/sahamBonus and 40 hmetd/tanpaHmetd
ledger rows against independently-derived Track 2 transition dates:

| action type | n matched | median lag | range |
|---|---|---|---|
| stockSplit | 58 | 0 days | -4..0 |
| hmetd (rights issue) | 40 | 19 days | 11..84 |
| sahamBonus | 13 | 18 days | 10..24 |
| Dividen Saham | 7 | 21 days | 16..24 |
| tanpaHmetd | 2 | 25 days | 6..44 |

Clean and type-dependent, not messy: splits list essentially same-day as
their ex-date; rights issues, bonus shares, and stock dividends all lag
by a consistent ~2.5-3 weeks (real settlement/listing delay). The median
lag is never applied to invent an ex_date for a row that doesn't have
one independently established — see the source module's docstring point
1. `reverseStock` has zero matched samples (only 5 rows total, all
pre-2020) — assumed to behave like `stockSplit` on mechanical grounds,
NOT empirically confirmed.

**Ratio only derived for stockSplit/reverseStock/sahamBonus** —
`after / (after - added)`, validated exactly against TPIA (4.0,
confirmed 1:4) and BBNI (2.0, confirmed 1:2). `hmetd`/`tanpaHmetd` never
get a derived ratio (COCO's real 2026-07-28 row derives ~1.037x against
an announced 1:3 factor 4 (~1.33x) — reflects shares actually taken up,
not the offer terms).

## THE BIG FINDING — Yahoo's `close_raw` is not reliably raw

This is the reason Phase 2 grew a large sub-investigation before closing.
It started as a single-ticker question (why do `FASW`'s Yahoo and IDX
prices disagree?) and turned into a systemic finding affecting hundreds
of tickers. Three distinct, independently-verified failure modes, not one:

1. **Yahoo retroactively applies stock splits even with
   `auto_adjust=False`.** This is a real, if under-documented, Yahoo
   behavior: their "unadjusted" `Close` excludes dividends but NOT
   splits — the entire historical series gets rescaled the moment a
   split happens, silently. ~106 tickers affected in our data (Tier 1 of
   the review file). Verified concretely via `PTRO`: IDX's raw feed shows
   close jumping 27,450 → 2,880 in one day (2025-01-03, -89.5%) with
   volume exploding 17x (7.5M → 126.5M shares) — the unambiguous
   signature of a real 1-for-10 split. Yahoo's close was already on the
   post-split scale for the *entire* prior history.
2. **Yahoo has had at least 3 multi-day, market-wide outages since 2020**
   (2022-03-11/14, 2024-01-12/15, 2024-03-21/26 — 85 to 140 tickers each),
   **unrelated to any real IDX event.** Proven from our own data, not
   inferred: IDX's market breadth (tickers with non-zero volume, total
   value traded, vs. trailing 20-day median) is completely normal
   (94-109% of baseline) during all three windows. The positive control —
   the real March 2020 COVID-crash trading halt — shows a genuine 155%
   value-traded spike on the actual halt day. Same detection signature
   (many tickers, Yahoo volume=0, IDX moving normally) but only one of
   the four has a real cause behind it.
3. **Yahoo silently freezes individual tickers for extended runs, with no
   other visible defect.** `BNBR`: Yahoo shows a flat 3800.0 for 767
   days (2020-01-02 → 2023-02-15) while IDX shows continuous real trading
   underneath (738/767 days with non-zero volume — corrected 2026-08-11,
   was mistyped 738/738 — actual price range 50-91 — Yahoo's frozen value
   is 42-76x the real price, not just stale, wrong). `ADMF`: flat 289
   days, same shape. The run-length distribution across all affected
   tickers is bottom-loaded — median 3 days, 90 of 106 isolated-freeze
   runs are 2-5 days. Long freezes like BNBR/ADMF are the dramatic
   examples; short ones are the common case and the easiest to miss,
   which is exactly why `jobs/validate.py::check_frozen_price_divergence`
   (the `full-history-audit` command) exists now as a standing check.
   Caroline independently re-examined BNBR against a TradingView chart
   (2026-08-11) showing it flat ~48 from late 2018 to late 2022 and
   hypothesized suspension, not real trading, worried the "738/767 with
   volume" figure was actually the Phase 1b zero-trade sentinel
   (open/high/low nulled, close carried forward on volume=0 days) being
   misread. Checked directly: only 29 of 767 days are that sentinel; the
   other 738 are IDX-reported nonzero volume, up to 253,758,500 shares in
   a single day. Real trading, not sentinel — but 626 of those 767 days
   (82%) close at *exactly* 50.0000, which is IDX's minimum tick price
   ("gocap"). Both observations were correct at once: the price genuinely
   was flat (that's what TradingView shows), because BNBR was genuinely
   pinned at the exchange floor with real, often huge, matched volume —
   not suspended. See AT_FLOOR below; this reclassified 16 tickers, not
   just BNBR (BNBR's own classification as an isolated Yahoo freeze,
   category B, was correct all along and did not change).

## CONSEQUENCE (decision, not a note)

**`close_adj` and return-series work are fine as-is.** The split-retroactive-
adjustment behavior that broke `close_raw` is *exactly* what makes
`close_adj` usable — Yahoo doing that adjustment is the point of that
column. **`close_raw` from Yahoo is unreliable for any level-based work**:
tick size, ARA/ARB band checks, gap detection, price-based liquidity
filters. Per CLAUDE.md's new SOURCE AUTHORITY convention: IDX is
authoritative for `close_raw` (2020-01-02 onward), Yahoo is authoritative
for `close_adj`. **Phase 4's feature builder must be explicit about which
column, and which source, each feature reads** — this is not optional
follow-up, it's a design constraint the feature builder has to satisfy
from its first line of code.

**Pre-2020 Yahoo history: downgraded from "exploration-only" to NOT USABLE
for quantitative work.** Previously this repo's stance was "kept locally
for research, excluded from production scope." That's no longer strong
enough — no second source exists before 2020-01-02 (IDX's own retention
wall, see Phase 1b above), so split contamination and silent freezes in
that period are **both undetectable and unfixable**. There is no way to
know whether any given pre-2020 Yahoo value is trustworthy. Treat it as
opt-in-with-explicit-acknowledgment-of-risk, not opt-out.

## B-BUCKET AUDIT: AT_FLOOR + MATERIALITY (2026-08-11)

Follow-up to THE BIG FINDING above, triggered by two external
confirmations from Caroline plus her own re-examination of the BNBR
conclusion (see item 3's addendum above for the BNBR-specific story).
Two distinct classifier gaps found and fixed in
`jobs/classify_discrepancies.py`, both in the frozen-run (Track 1) and
regime-transition (Track 2) detectors:

**AT_FLOOR** — Rp50 is IDX's minimum tick price ("gocap"). A ticker can
trade there for months or years, with real (sometimes huge) volume,
purely because the exchange mechanically won't let the price go lower.
A naive "close never changes" check can't tell that apart from a real
freeze. This is not a rare edge case: **303 of 989 IDX tickers (31%)
have spent at least one real-volume day at exactly Rp50; 158 (16%)
spent more than 10% of their history there; 82 (8.3%) spent more than a
quarter; 11 (1.1%) spent more than half.** Worth remembering for Phase
3/4 liquidity filtering generally, not just this classifier — see the
Phase 4 note below. `IDX_FLOOR_PRICE` / `FrozenRun.at_floor` implement
the exception, IDX-side only (Yahoo isn't authoritative for `close_raw`
— CLAUDE.md SOURCE AUTHORITY — so a Yahoo close of exactly 50 proves
nothing and gets no exception). Reclassified **77 frozen runs across 16
tickers** (ABBA, AHAP, BBRM, BEEF, BEKS, BGTG, BKSL, BRMS, MEJA, MMIX,
MTWI, PANR, PGJO, WEHA, YELO, ZBRA) from category C ("both sides stale,
not a data-quality defect") into A/B. `BBRM` is the clearest case: IDX
bit-identical at exactly 50 for a real 522-day run (2020-01-02 →
2022-02-17, nonzero volume 298 of 522 days, up to 1.59M shares/day)
while Yahoo sat frozen at a completely unrelated 67.7745 for the *same
522 days* — a genuine 2+-year Yahoo defect that "both sides frozen" had
been silently filing as a non-finding. This is CLAUDE.md's 5th
confirmed silent-success instance.

**MATERIALITY** — a side whose close value changes is not automatically
"moving." The B-bucket audit found **10 tickers** (ASRM x2, FASW, WICO,
BRNA, DSSA, PEGE, TFCO, LCKM, TRUS, CTBN) filed as category B (isolated
Yahoo freeze, IDX genuinely trading) where the "movement" was really
1-3 tiny (100-1200 share) trades scattered through an otherwise fully
zero-volume run, ticking between just 2-3 distinct values. ASRM's
clearest run (2023-09-26 → 11-29, 46 days): only 10 of 46 days have any
volume at all, and just 2 distinct close values the whole time — not
meaningfully different from LCKM, independently already on record in
remaining thread 3 below as a suspected suspension. Same gap in the
mirror direction: FASW's 86-day, WIKA's 73-day, and FISH's 153-day
`frozen_side='idx'` runs had Yahoo "moving" on just 1-2 real-volume days
out of the whole window. Fix: a side's nonzero-volume-day fraction over
the run must clear `MATERIALITY_THRESHOLD` (default 0.5) before it
counts as genuinely active — applied to both Track 1 (frozen runs) and
Track 2 (regime transitions, replacing a single-day volume check with a
window-fraction check across the post-regime).

**Sensitivity swept at 0.0/0.3/0.5/0.7 before picking 0.5** (requested
explicitly — wanted to see whether the answer was stable or knife-edge
before committing to a number): category B goes 108→108→105→103
tickers and C goes 159→163→166→174 across that range — a ~5% shift end
to end, no cliff at any single threshold. Why: of 656 frozen runs with
a non-inert "other side", 590 (90%) sit at exactly 1.0 volume-day
fraction (fully real trading every day) and the remaining 66 are
thinly spread across 0.0-0.9 with no secondary cluster anywhere — no
natural gap to land a threshold on, so 0.5 (the midpoint of that flat
region) is as defensible as any other value in [0.3, 0.7]. Not
knife-edge; revisit only if a future audit finds a real cluster near
the boundary. `materiality_threshold` is a parameter on `classify()` /
`find_frozen_runs()` / `find_regime_transitions()` and a `--materiality-
threshold` CLI flag on `classify_discrepancies.py`, not hardcoded.

Regression tests for both fixes, same pattern as the ADMF noise-floor
fix: `tests/test_classify_discrepancies.py` (9 tests total, 6 new).

**Not yet done**: this pass only reclassified existing frozen-run /
regime-transition populations under the corrected logic. It did not
re-run `jobs/reconcile.py`'s full-history scan or re-persist
`price_discrepancies` / re-generate `review/corporate_actions_candidates.md`
under the new classifier — Tier 1/2/3 in the review file still reflect
the pre-fix classification. Do that before the next tier-by-tier review
session, not silently assume the review file already reflects this.

## Decisions and why (the part compaction destroys)

- **`prices_daily`'s PK was widened to include `ingested_at`, rejecting a
  companion audit-table design.** The audit-table alternative's failure
  mode is *silent*: a reader that forgets to merge the side table gets
  back one plausible-looking row containing the future-corrected value,
  no error, no duplicate, no signal anything's wrong. The widened-PK
  approach's failure mode is *loud*: get the write path wrong and you get
  duplicate rows per (ticker, date) — trivially catchable with a
  one-line assertion (`tests/test_prices_daily_revisions.py` has one).
  Loud beats silent (see CLAUDE.md's SILENT SUCCESS standing instruction
  — this decision is instance #3 of the four listed there).
- **`daily.py` writes a new `prices_daily` row ONLY on an actual value
  change**, never unconditionally. The rolling 7-day window re-touches
  the same days every run; blind appending would add ~7 days × ~962
  tickers × 2 sources ≈ 13k rows/day that record no new information.
- **2020-01-02 is the honest modeling window, not ~2010 as originally
  hoped.** IDX's `GetStockSummary` endpoint has no data before that date
  — verified empirically (every 2010-2019 date probed returns
  `recordsTotal: 0` on a clean 200 OK), not a bug or a rate limit.
  Consequence: delisted tickers are only discoverable from 2020 onward.
  Pre-2020 Yahoo history's status was further downgraded on 2026-08-09 —
  see "CONSEQUENCE" above — from exploration-only to not usable at all,
  once split-contamination and silent freezes turned out to be
  undetectable there (no second source to check against).
- **IDX is authoritative for `close_raw`, Yahoo for `close_adj` (decided
  2026-08-09).** Not a per-case judgment call — a standing policy, because
  the failure modes behind it (retroactive split adjustment, multi-day
  outages, silent freezes) are systemic to Yahoo's raw-price feed, not
  occasional. See THE BIG FINDING above for the evidence.
- **Suspensions must NOT go into `known_issues`.** That table is for
  permanent, understood defects (spec-external facts that won't change:
  the 2007 Yahoo feed anomaly, a ticker's structurally sparse history).
  A suspension is temporary and ends; suppressing it permanently would
  hide a real re-listing. This is why remaining thread 3 proposes a
  separate `ticker_status_daily` concept instead.
- **Survivorship bias is documented, not laundered.** `securities` is
  962 active + 27 delisted (Phase 1b), explicitly biased for anything
  delisted before 2020-01-01. The README and this file say so in plain
  terms.
- **The corporate-actions review file is tiered by verification strength
  with per-row confidence, not a flat list.** Nothing gets applied to
  `corporate_actions` without human sign-off, tier by tier — a wrong
  corporate action is worse than a missing one (it would silently corrupt
  the adjustment basis for every downstream reader).

## Gotchas

- **Postgres `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` still performs a speculative
  insertion internally, even on a true no-op conflict.** Cost `securities`
  (989 rows) 84MB of dead-tuple bloat from ~1.6M redundant harvester
  calls before it was caught and fixed with a known-tickers cache. Regular
  `VACUUM`/autovacuum reclaims dead-tuple space for *reuse*, it does
  **not** shrink the file — only `VACUUM FULL` does that, and it takes an
  exclusive lock. Watch for this pattern anywhere a "does this already
  exist, insert if not" check runs at high frequency against a
  mostly-already-populated table.
- **A "frozen run" detector needs its threshold applied to the FULL
  window, not just past a noise floor.** A short coincidental repeat on
  the side that's actually moving normally (e.g. two days landing on the
  same price by chance) can clear the same low length threshold the real
  long freeze clears on the other side, causing a false "both sides
  frozen" classification that hides the real, single-sided defect. Found
  via `ADMF` (would have been misfiled into the zero-volume/suspension
  bucket, invisible). Locked in with a regression test in
  `tests/test_classify_discrepancies.py`.
- **A ratio-based anomaly detector needs a plausibility bound on the
  ratio itself, not just a near-zero-denominator guard.** `BCIC`: Yahoo
  carried a literal 5,000,000 close for 6 days — a corrupted value, not a
  price — with a completely normal (non-near-zero) IDX-side denominator,
  so a denominator guard alone does not catch it. Separately, `DSSA`
  showed why you also need to verify your own methodology's core
  assumption holds per-case: the detector assumed "ratio converges back
  to ~1.0 after a real transition," but DSSA's flagged transition never
  actually converged — it was a partial artifact of a much larger,
  pre-existing mismatch, not a resolved split. Both are now excluded with
  logged reasons in the review file rather than silently dropped or
  wrongly included.
- **Railway cron runs in UTC.** Relevant whenever Railway actually gets
  provisioned (thread 6) — spec's "18:30 WIB (11:30 UTC)" schedule needs
  the UTC figure there, not the WIB one. (launchd, by contrast, uses the
  machine's local system timezone — see the caveat baked into
  `launchd/com.idx.daily.plist` itself.)
- **launchd, not cron, on macOS** — specifically because
  `StartCalendarInterval` runs a missed job when the machine wakes from
  sleep, which plain cron does not. A laptop that's asleep at 18:30 WIB
  every day would silently never run under cron.
